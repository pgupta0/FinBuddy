# Real-World PMS and Family Office Strategies (Growth-oriented / Aggressive tiers)

This document covers how the highest-conviction professionally managed strategies — Portfolio
Management Services (PMS) and family offices — actually invest, as a real-world illustration for
users at the Growth-oriented or Aggressive end of the risk-profile spectrum. Read alongside the
important access caveat at the end: most of what's described here is not directly available to a
typical retail investor at a small investment size.

## Portfolio Management Services (PMS) — what they actually are

A PMS is a professionally managed, individually-held equity portfolio (unlike a mutual fund, the
investor owns the actual underlying shares directly in their own account, managed on their behalf).
SEBI mandates a minimum investment of **₹50 lakh** to open a PMS account — this was raised over
time from ₹5 lakh (1993) to ₹25 lakh, and then to ₹50 lakh in November 2019, specifically to keep
PMS reserved for investors with substantial capital and higher risk tolerance. Source:
[IIFL Knowledge Center — What is the Minimum Ticket Size for
PMS?](https://www.indiainfoline.com/knowledge-center/pms/what-is-the-minimum-ticket-size-for-pms).

### Motilal Oswal PMS — the "QGLP" philosophy

Motilal Oswal's PMS strategies are built around a **Quality, Growth, Longevity, Price (QGLP)**
research philosophy — a fundamentals-based approach to picking businesses with strong,
sustainable competitive positions. Rather than one single strategy, they offer several named
strategies with different focuses, real and currently marketed:

- **Founders Portfolio** — targets companies where founder ownership exceeds 26%, on the thesis
  that founder-led businesses tend to compound value more consistently over the long run.
- **Next Trillion Dollar Opportunity** — positioned around sectors expected to benefit from India's
  next phase of economic growth.
- **Value Migration Strategy** — focused on compounding through well-managed, quality businesses
  held for extended periods.
- **Mid to Mega Strategy** — pursues long-term capital appreciation across market-cap segments.
- **India Growth Fund** — focused on quality businesses with strong management for long-term
  compounding.

Source: [Motilal Oswal Asset Management — PMS](https://www.motilaloswalamc.com/pms). A recurring,
real, and verifiable feature across these: Motilal Oswal states that its own fund managers and
leadership invest personal capital in the same strategies offered to clients.

### Marcellus Consistent Compounders Portfolio (CCP)

Marcellus's flagship PMS strategy is deliberately **concentrated — around 20 stocks**, selected
from an initial screened universe of roughly 50 candidates. The real, stated philosophy: hold
"heavily moated companies" with high Return on Capital Employed (RoCE) that consistently reinvest
profits to grow, favoring businesses whose value comes from intellectual assets (brands, networks,
processes) over physical/fixed assets. The methodology is two-stage: a quantitative screen to build
the initial universe, followed by bottom-up fundamental research on competitive moats. Source:
[Marcellus Investment Managers — Consistent Compounders
Portfolio](https://marcellus.in/portfolio-management-services/marcellus-consistent-compounder-pms/).
This illustrates a real, very different flavor of high-conviction investing from Motilal Oswal's
multi-strategy suite above: extreme concentration in a small number of "quality compounder"
businesses, rather than a broader, more diversified high-conviction book.

## Family offices — how the wealthiest investors actually allocate

Family offices manage the wealth of a single ultra-high-net-worth family, and their real,
documented allocation trends (2025–2026) show a structural shift:

- **40–45%** of many Indian family office portfolios now goes into **alternatives** — private
  equity, venture capital, private credit, Alternative Investment Funds (AIFs), REITs, and InvITs.
- Dedicated allocations of **10–20% or more specifically to private equity and venture capital**
  are increasingly common.
- Family offices were **traditionally concentrated in domestic equities, fixed income, and real
  estate**, and are now actively diversifying toward global and alternative exposure.

Source: [Business Today — Indian family offices are putting up to 45% of their portfolios into
alternatives](https://www.businesstoday.in/latest/corporate/story/indian-family-offices-are-putting-up-to-45-of-their-portfolios-into-alternatives-where-is-the-money-going-552692-2026-09-02),
citing the **EY–Julius Baer Indian Family Office Playbook: Now, Next and Beyond**. A companion EY
report notes that a majority of family offices (57%) still keep private-market exposure (PE/VC)
under 10% of the portfolio even while broadening into these newer asset classes, suggesting the
shift is real but generally measured rather than wholesale. Source: [EY — 25% of family offices
prioritise asset preservation, but eye global, alternative
investments](https://www.ey.com/en_in/newsroom/2025/06/25-percent-of-family-offices-prioritise-asset-preservation-but-eye-global-alternative-investments-ey-julius-baer-report).

## The access caveat — read this before comparing a user's own portfolio here

Both PMS and the alternatives family offices use (AIFs, private equity, venture capital) have real,
regulatory minimum investment thresholds that put them out of reach for most first-time investors:

- **PMS: ₹50 lakh minimum** (SEBI-mandated, see above).
- **AIFs (all three categories): ₹1 crore minimum per investor** — a statutory, non-negotiable SEBI
  requirement, with the sole exception of Angel Funds (a Category I sub-type) at ₹25 lakh. Source:
  [DealPlexus — AIFs in India: Categories, Minimum Tickets, Taxation, and Portfolio
  Fit](https://www.dealplexus.com/blog/aifs-india-guide).

When presenting this material to a user at the Growth-oriented or Aggressive tier, treat it as
**illustrative of the philosophy and structure** used at the highest-conviction end of investing
(concentration, quality/moat focus, growing alternatives exposure) — not as something the user can
directly replicate at typical retail investment sizes. The realistic, accessible version of the
same ideas for a retail investor is the mutual-fund-level real examples in the companion
"real-world allocation benchmarks" document (e.g. a flexi-cap fund's value-investing approach, or a
multi-asset fund's aggressive benchmark), and the endowment-style strategy explainer, which
deliberately adapts the institutional diversification philosophy into retail-accessible building
blocks.
